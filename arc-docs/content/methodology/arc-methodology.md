# ARC Methodology

## Purpose

The ARC Methodology is the standard process used to understand reality before designing systems.

It exists to reduce assumptions, increase confidence, and ensure every solution is grounded in evidence rather than opinion.

Every engagement, product, and internal initiative follows this methodology.

---

The cycle never ends.

Learning from reality begins the next cycle of discovery.

---

# Phase 01 — Observe

## Objective

Understand reality without attempting to solve it.

## Activities

- Observe people
- Observe workflows
- Observe decisions
- Observe information movement
- Observe constraints
- Observe behaviours

Questions:

What actually happens?

What is different from what people believe happens?

---

# Phase 02 — Discover

## Objective

Identify relationships.

Everything operates inside systems.

Look for:

- Patterns
- Dependencies
- Bottlenecks
- Feedback loops
- Signals
- Opportunities

The objective is understanding.

Not fixing.

---

# Phase 03 — Challenge

## Objective

Test every important assumption.

Ask:

Why?

What evidence supports this?

What contradicts it?

What happens if the opposite is true?

Never protect assumptions.

Protect truth.

---

# Phase 04 — Lock Truth

## Objective

Validate findings using evidence.

Truth should be:

- Observable
- Repeatable
- Explainable
- Supported by evidence

Once validated, truth becomes the foundation for design.

---

# Phase 05 — Design

## Objective

Design the system.

Begin with:

Purpose

↓

People

↓

Relationships

↓

Processes

↓

Information

↓

Technology

Technology is the final implementation layer.

---

# Phase 06 — Validate

## Objective

Test the design before large-scale implementation.

Questions:

Does it solve the original problem?

Does it reduce complexity?

Does it improve capability?

Does it introduce unnecessary risk?

Reality decides.

---

# Phase 07 — Build

## Objective

Implement only validated designs.

Build:

- Clearly
- Securely
- Incrementally
- With documentation
- With measurable outcomes

---

# Phase 08 — Measure

## Objective

Determine whether the system achieved its intended outcome.

Measure:

- Performance
- Adoption
- Capability
- Efficiency
- Quality
- Risk reduction

If outcomes don't improve, revisit the design.

---

# Phase 09 — Learn

## Objective

Capture knowledge.

Every project must improve:

- Documentation
- Methodology
- Templates
- Decision quality
- Future delivery

Knowledge compounds over time.

---

# Governing Principles

Reality over opinion.

Systems over technology.

Evidence over assumption.

Capability over dependency.

Learning over certainty.

Long-term value over short-term optimisation.

---

# ARC Definition of Success

A successful project is one that leaves:

The client stronger.

The methodology better.

The knowledge richer.

The next project easier.

The organisation more capable.
