# LAW 001 — Responsibility

Version: 1.0.0  
Status: Ratified  
Authority: HOLY ARC Constitution  
Classification: Internal

---

# Law

> Every responsibility shall have exactly one constitutional owner.

Execution may be delegated.

Ownership and accountability may not be silently transferred.

---

# Purpose

Establish clear ownership, accountability and boundaries for every responsibility within the DYS96 ecosystem.

---

# Derived Rules

## R001.1 — Single Ownership

Every responsibility must have exactly one constitutional owner.

Two systems must not own the same primary responsibility.

---

## R001.2 — Accountability

The constitutional owner remains accountable for:

- Integrity
- Correctness
- Outcomes
- Governance
- Continuous improvement
- Compliance with constitutional authority

Accountability remains with the owner even when execution is delegated.

---

## R001.3 — Delegation

Execution may be delegated through a governed relationship.

Every delegation must identify:

- Responsibility owner
- Delegated actor
- Permitted actions
- Boundaries
- Duration
- Evidence requirements
- Review conditions

Delegation does not transfer constitutional ownership.

---

## R001.4 — No Responsibility Drift

A system must not silently assume another system’s responsibility.

Responsibility changes require:

- Evidence
- Impact assessment
- Constitutional review
- Explicit approval
- Updated documentation
- Preserved history

Implementation changes do not automatically change ownership.

---

## R001.5 — Explicit Boundaries

Every constitutional system must define:

- What it owns
- What it does not own
- What it may delegate
- What requires approval
- Which relationships it maintains

Undefined responsibility is prohibited.

---

## R001.6 — Responsibility Before Implementation

Responsibility must be defined before:

- Standards
- Specifications
- Contracts
- Architecture
- Engineering
- Automation
- Operations

Implementation must reflect constitutional ownership.

Implementation must not determine constitutional ownership.

---

## R001.7 — Relationship Ownership

Relationships must also have accountable ownership.

Relationship ownership does not replace the ownership of participating systems.

The relationship owner is accountable for:

- Integrity
- Permitted flow
- Compliance
- Evidence
- Failure handling
- Lifecycle management

---

## R001.8 — Conflict Resolution

When two systems claim the same responsibility:

1. Record the conflict.
2. Preserve supporting evidence.
3. Identify the constitutional purpose of the responsibility.
4. Review existing ownership and boundaries.
5. Assess affected relationships.
6. Select one constitutional owner.
7. Update dependent documents and systems.
8. Preserve the decision history.

One responsibility must leave the process with one owner.

---

# Constitutional Tests

A responsibility complies with LAW 001 only when:

- Its purpose is defined.
- It has exactly one owner.
- Accountability is explicit.
- Boundaries are documented.
- Delegation is traceable.
- Relationships are governed.
- No competing ownership exists.
- Changes preserve history.

---

# Violations

The following violate LAW 001:

- Multiple owners for one responsibility
- Undefined ownership
- Assumed ownership
- Silent responsibility transfer
- Ownership inferred only from implementation
- Delegation without accountability
- Systems operating outside their boundaries
- Responsibility changes without constitutional review

# Audit Criteria

LAW 001 passes audit when:

- Every responsibility has one identifiable owner.
- Every system has explicit boundaries.
- Delegation records preserve accountability.
- No responsibility overlap exists.
- Ownership changes are governed and traceable.

---

# Final Principle

> Ownership creates accountability. Delegation enables execution. Neither may be confused with the other.
