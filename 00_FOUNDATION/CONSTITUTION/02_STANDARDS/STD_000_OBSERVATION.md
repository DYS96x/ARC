# Standard

> Every ARC process shall begin by observing reality before creating assumptions, solutions or implementation.

Observation is the first interface between reality and the ecosystem.

---

# Purpose

Define how ARC observes reality in a consistent, evidence-aware and repeatable way.

Observation exists to:

- Reduce assumptions.
- Discover actual conditions.
- Identify signals.
- Understand relationships.
- Establish context.
- Prepare evidence collection.

# Observation Principles

## O001 — Observe Before Assuming

ARC shall seek observable reality before forming conclusions.

Assumptions must remain identifiable.

---

## O002 — Context Matters

Every observation should include:

- Time
- Location
- Environment
- Conditions
- Participants
- Relationships
- Relevant history

An observation without context may be incomplete.

---

## O003 — Separate Observation From Interpretation

Observation describes what occurred.

Interpretation explains what it may mean.

They must remain separate.

Example:

Observation:

"System response time increased from 200ms to 900ms."

Interpretation:

"The database may be causing performance degradation."

The interpretation requires further evidence.

---

## O004 — Multiple Perspectives

Where practical, ARC should consider:

- Direct observation
- System data
- Human experience
- Historical evidence
- External context

A single perspective may be incomplete.

---

## O005 — Record Uncertainty

Observations may contain:

- Known information
- Unknown information
- Assumptions
- Questions

Uncertainty should be visible.

---

## O006 — Preserve Original Observation

Original observations should remain unchanged.

Later interpretations may evolve.

The original observation remains historical evidence.

# Observation Quality Tests

An observation passes when:

- It describes reality before interpretation.
- Context is recorded.
- Sources are identifiable.
- Uncertainty is visible.
- Historical record is preserved.
- Future assessment is possible.

---

# Violations

The following violate this standard:

- Starting with assumptions.
- Presenting interpretation as observation.
- Removing context.
- Hiding uncertainty.
- Altering original observations without history.
- Making decisions without sufficient observation.
