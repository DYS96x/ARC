# STD_003 — Relationship

Version: 1.0.0  
Status: Draft  
Authority: HOLY ARC Laws  
Classification: Internal

---

# Standard

> Every meaningful interaction within the ARC ecosystem shall be represented as a governed relationship with defined purpose, ownership, boundaries and behaviour.

Relationships are not connections.

Relationships are capabilities.

---

# Purpose

Define how ARC systems, objects, people and processes interact while maintaining:

- Accountability
- Security
- Traceability
- Adaptability
- Constitutional integrity

---

# Relationship To Laws

This standard derives from:

## LAW 001 — Responsibility

Every relationship requires accountable ownership.

## LAW 002 — Evidence

Significant relationship activity should produce evidence.

## LAW 003 — Relationships

All interactions occur through governed relationships.

## LAW 005 — State

Relationships have lifecycle and operational state.

## LAW 006 — Security

Relationships operate within trust boundaries.

## LAW 007 — Change

Relationships evolve through controlled change.

---

# Relationship Definition

A relationship is a governed connection between two or more constitutional objects.

A relationship defines:

- Why interaction exists.
- Who participates.
- What may flow.
- What authority exists.
- What evidence is produced.

---

# Relationship Identity

Every relationship requires:

- Unique identifier
- Relationship type
- Version
- Creation record
- Current status

A relationship must remain identifiable throughout its lifecycle.

---

# Relationship Purpose

Every relationship must answer:

- Why does this relationship exist?
- What capability does it enable?
- What outcome does it support?

Relationships without purpose create unnecessary complexity.

---

# Participants

Every relationship must identify:

- Source participant
- Destination participant
- Supporting participants where required

Participants may include:

- Systems
- Objects
- People
- Services
- External organisations

---

# Relationship Ownership

Every relationship requires one accountable owner.

The owner is responsible for:

- Integrity
- Lifecycle
- Compliance
- Evolution
- Review

Relationship ownership does not transfer system ownership.

---

# Flow Rules

Every relationship must define:

## Allowed Flow

What information, decisions, resources or signals may move.

## Restricted Flow

What must not move.

## Direction

How interaction occurs.

## Conditions

When interaction is permitted.

Undefined flow is prohibited.

---

# Permissions

Relationships shall define:

- Who may initiate.
- Who may receive.
- Who may modify.
- Who may approve.
- Who may observe.

Permission must follow constitutional authority.

---

# Relationship State

Relationships shall maintain governed state:

```text
Proposed

↓

Approved

↓

Active

↓

Modified

↓

Deprecated

↓

Retired
```

---

# Relationship Evidence

Where appropriate, relationships should produce evidence including:

- Interaction record
- Decision record
- Change record
- Outcome record
- Audit record

Evidence must remain attributable.

---

# Relationship Failure

Relationships must define:

- Expected failures.
- Detection method.
- Recovery behaviour.
- Escalation path.
- Review process.

Failure shall not silently bypass constitutional boundaries.

---

# Relationship Evolution

Relationship changes require:

- Impact assessment.
- Version update.
- Compatibility review.
- Historical preservation.

---

# Relationship Quality Tests

A relationship passes this standard when:

- Purpose is clear.
- Participants are known.
- Ownership is explicit.
- Flow is governed.
- Permissions exist.
- Evidence is possible.
- Lifecycle is defined.
- Failure handling exists.

---

# Violations

The following violate this standard:

- Hidden relationships.
- Undefined dependencies.
- Uncontrolled data flow.
- Relationships without owners.
- Bypassing system boundaries.
- Silent relationship changes.
- Relationships that cannot be audited.
