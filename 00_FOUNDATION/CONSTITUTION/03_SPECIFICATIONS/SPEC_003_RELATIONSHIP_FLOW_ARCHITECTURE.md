# SPEC_003 — Relationship & Flow Architecture

Version: 1.0.0  
Status: Draft  
Authority: HOLY ARC Standards  
Classification: Internal

---

# Specification

> Every ARC ecosystem interaction shall be represented as a governed relationship with adaptive flow, explicit ownership, defined boundaries and continuous feedback.

The ecosystem is a living network.

It is not a linear pipeline.

---

# Purpose

Define how information, decisions, resources, permissions and feedback move through ARC.

This specification establishes:

- Relationship architecture
- Flow behaviour
- Feedback mechanisms
- System interaction rules
- Ecosystem evolution patterns

---

## LAW 003 — Relationships

Relationships and flows must evolve safely.

# Flow Definition

Flow is the movement of:

- Information
- Evidence
- Decisions
- Resources
- Permissions
- Signals
- Feedback
- Context

through governed relationships.

---

# Flow Characteristics

Every flow must define:

## Source

Where does the flow originate?

---

## Destination

Where does the flow move?

---

## Purpose

Why does the flow exist?

---

## Authority

Who permits the flow?

---

## Conditions

When is the flow allowed?

---

## Feedback

How does the receiving system respond?

---

# Bidirectional Architecture

ARC relationships should support:

```text
Request

↓

Response

↓

Observation

↓

Feedback

↓

Improvement
```

A system should not become isolated after providing an output.

---

# Relationship Types

ARC recognises:

---

## Information Relationship

Purpose:

Exchange understanding.

Examples:

- Reports
- Knowledge transfer
- Documentation

---

## Decision Relationship

Purpose:

Exchange approvals and decisions.

Examples:

- Governance
- Reviews
- Authorisations

---

## Resource Relationship

Purpose:

Exchange capability.

Examples:

- Infrastructure
- Services
- Tools

---

## Learning Relationship

Purpose:

Exchange improvement.

Examples:

- Feedback
- Lessons
- Patterns

---

## Trust Relationship

Purpose:

Establish permitted interaction.

Examples:

- Identity
- Permissions
- Security boundaries

---

# Flow Rules

Every flow must define:

```text
What moves?

Who can move it?

Why does it move?

When can it move?

Where is it recorded?

How does it improve?
```

---

# Feedback Loop Architecture

Every meaningful relationship should support:

```text
Action

↓

Outcome

↓

Observation

↓

Evidence

↓

Learning

↓

Updated Behaviour
```

Feedback is not an error condition.

Feedback is the mechanism of evolution.

---

# State-Aware Flow

Flow behaviour may change based on state.

Example:

```text
Normal State

↓

Standard Flow


Risk State

↓

Restricted Flow


Recovery State

↓

Restoration Flow
```

---

# Flow Security

Every flow requires:

- Identity validation
- Authority verification
- Permission checking
- Audit capability

---

# Flow History

Important flows should preserve:

- Origin
- Destination
- Time
- Purpose
- Outcome
- Changes

History allows reconstruction of ecosystem behaviour.

---

# Flow Failure

Every important relationship must define:

- Failure detection
- Containment
- Recovery
- Escalation
- Learning

# Validation Criteria

The Relationship & Flow Architecture passes when:

- Relationships are explicit.
- Ownership is clear.
- Flows are governed.
- Feedback exists.
- Security boundaries exist.
- History is preserved.
- Systems can evolve.

---

# Violations

The following violate this specification:

- One-way critical communication.
- Hidden dependencies.
- Undefined data movement.
- Unowned relationships.
- Missing feedback loops.
- Flow bypassing governance.

---

# Final Principle

> Pipelines move information. Ecosystems evolve through relationships.
